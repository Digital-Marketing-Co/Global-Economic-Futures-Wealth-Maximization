# Research and Publication Standard

## Source hierarchy

1. National statistical offices, central banks, ministries, customs authorities, regulators, and audited public records
2. IMF, World Bank, UN agencies, WTO, BIS, OECD, ILO, regional development banks, and official regional statistical bodies
3. Peer-reviewed research and transparently documented institutional datasets
4. High-quality independent analysis used for interpretation, never as a silent substitute for primary data

## Required provenance

Every material datum or claim records publisher, dataset or document title, table or series identifier, URL, publication/update date, access date, geography, period, frequency, units, currency and price basis, seasonal adjustment, methodology version, revision/vintage, license, and transformation.

## Reconciliation rules

- Never merge series solely because labels resemble one another.
- Preserve competing official estimates when definitions differ.
- Explain breaks in series, rebasing, redenomination, boundary changes, and exceptional revisions.
- Distinguish nominal from real, gross from net, stocks from flows, residence from nationality, and calendar from fiscal periods.
- Label estimates, imputations, nowcasts, forecasts, and scenarios visibly.

## Forecasting rules

- Use ensembles appropriate to the series and data history rather than one universal model.
- Compare against naive and consensus benchmarks with rolling-origin validation.
- Publish assumptions, backtests, prediction intervals, revision sensitivity, and failure modes.
- Treat 100-year results as conditional scenarios with widening uncertainty, not precise forecasts.
- Do not infer causality from correlation without a defensible identification strategy.

## Mathematical signal families

Growth and acceleration; output gaps; inflation momentum and diffusion; real-rate and yield-curve signals; credit impulses; debt dynamics; fiscal impulse; external financing gaps; reserve adequacy; real effective exchange-rate misalignment; terms-of-trade shocks; trade concentration and complexity; productivity decomposition; demographic support ratios; inequality and wealth concentration; financial stress; regime shifts; anomaly detection; network centrality; and scenario sensitivity.

## Publication QA

- Claim-to-source ledger complete
- Page-local Chicago notes and deduplicated bibliography
- Equations followed by symbol, meaning, units/domain, and role tables where needed
- No invented values, citations, people, institutions, or claims
- Data tests for uniqueness, units, date ranges, missingness, outliers, and accounting identities
- Every PDF page rendered and inspected for clipping, overlap, broken equations, footnote collisions, and missing glyphs
- Motion and device orientation follow accessibility, privacy, and performance constraints
